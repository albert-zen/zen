## Five highest-impact findings

1. **P2: Recovery copy can be lost on reload.** Target: local uncertain-delivery card, 20–23. Trigger: application reload while the uncertain copy remains. Evidence: the UI explicitly says the local recovery copy is not saved across an app reload. Consequence: the user may lose the only visible recovery text, even though the delivery outcome is unresolved. Verification: persist the copy and its uncertain state across restart without automatically resending; run tests for both subsequently committed and uncommitted cases, and keep any retention/privacy policy explicit.
2. **P2: Check delivery has no distinguishable captured result.** Target: 21–22 “Check delivery.” Trigger: the manifest-reported check after opening details. Consequence: the user may be unsure whether anything happened and what the check actually establishes. The identical captures establish the lack of a durable _captured_ difference, not the absence of a transient feedback event or a successful internal check. Verification: record the entire click-to-settled sequence and expose progress plus a lasting, truthful result, including “not found yet; delivery still unconfirmed” where appropriate. Never infer non-delivery from an absence in a current read.
3. **P2: Pending tray overflow is easy to miss.** Target: 01–02. Trigger: two held steers and three queued inputs exceed tray height. Consequence: rows and controls below the edge can be overlooked; 02 shows that they become visible after scrolling. Verification: without prior instruction, ask participants to find all five inputs; expose overflow via a visible scroll bar, count/cue, or deliberate expansion, and test scrolling and controls with keyboard and pointer.
4. **P2: Running-send choices are dense and secondary labels are small.** Target: 13 send-action panel. Trigger: open the panel while a response is running. Consequence: users must differentiate queue, steer, each turn, interruption and stop using small explanatory text, increasing selection error risk. Verification: increase readable type/spacing or panel width and run first-use tests asking users to predict each operation and its shortcut; ensure the panel stays within the viewport at 200% zoom.
5. **P3: Held-reading state is implicit.** Target: 08–11. Trigger: scroll away from live output. Consequence: “Back to live” indicates an available destination but does not explicitly say the viewport is held or that new output continues. The sequence and receipts support that holding works; this is an affordance concern, not a claim the anchor failed. Verification: add concise “Reading position held” or equivalent next to the existing action, and test that users can identify both the state and how to resume without obscuring content.

## Strengths worth preserving

- Pending inputs are separated from admitted conversation content and assigned counts and timing labels; 18–19 also visibly distinguish local sending from the later canonical transcript.
- The uncertain-delivery copy explicitly warns against an unverified resend, offers details/check/recovery, and remains available after the separately edited recovery is sent (20–23). It does not make an unsupported definitive-failure claim.
- The older reading checkpoint remains in view in 08–11 while receipts show output continuing; “Back to live” offers an explicit return without forcing the reader to the bottom.

## Prioritized next iteration and guardrails

First preserve unresolved local recovery across reload with safe duplicate handling and add an honest, durable check result. Next expose tray overflow and improve the legibility and selection clarity of the running-send panel. Finally clarify the held-reading state and run keyboard, screen-reader, contrast, zoom and narrow-window tests.

Do not turn uncertain delivery into a definitive failure merely because the current canonical read lacks the message; do not automatically resend, remove the recovery copy after an unrelated edited send, merge pending inputs into the transcript, or force following when a user has deliberately scrolled away. Preserve distinctions between steer, queue and stop. Do not generalize these synthetic QA outcomes to real provider reliability.
